HUMANITARIAN OPERATIONS LEGAL FRAMEWORK
International Standards and Compliance Guidelines

INTRODUCTION
This document provides an overview of the legal and regulatory framework governing humanitarian operations, including international humanitarian law, human rights standards, and operational guidelines.

INTERNATIONAL HUMANITARIAN LAW (IHL)

Geneva Conventions and Additional Protocols:
The four Geneva Conventions (1949) and their Additional Protocols establish the legal framework for the protection of civilians, wounded combatants, and prisoners of war during armed conflict.

Key Principles:
1. Distinction: Parties must distinguish between civilians and combatants
2. Proportionality: Attacks must not cause excessive civilian harm
3. Precaution: All feasible precautions must be taken to avoid civilian casualties
4. Humanity: Persons not taking part in hostilities must be treated humanely

SPHERE HUMANITARIAN STANDARDS

The Sphere Handbook (2018 edition) sets minimum standards for humanitarian response across key sectors:

WASH Minimum Standards:
- Water supply: Minimum 15 liters per person per day
- Water quality: Free from contamination, acceptable taste, color, and odor
- Sanitation: Maximum 20 people per toilet
- Hygiene: Access to soap and hygiene items

Shelter and Settlement Standards:
- Covered living space: Minimum 3.5 square meters per person
- Household items: Cooking utensils, blankets, sleeping mats
- Thermal comfort: Protection from elements

Food Security and Nutrition Standards:
- General food distribution: 2,100 kcal per person per day
- Acute malnutrition treatment: Timely identification and treatment
- Micronutrient supplementation: Vitamin A, iron, iodine

Health Standards:
- Essential health services: Available and accessible to all
- Maternal and newborn health: Skilled birth attendance
- Communicable disease control: Vaccination, surveillance, outbreak response

UN SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT GOALS (SDGs)

Relevant SDGs for Humanitarian Action:
- SDG 1: No Poverty
- SDG 2: Zero Hunger
- SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being
- SDG 6: Clean Water and Sanitation
- SDG 10: Reduced Inequalities
- SDG 16: Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions

SAFEGUARDING AND PROTECTION

Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA):
All humanitarian workers must:
- Adhere to zero-tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse
- Report any concerns through safe reporting mechanisms
- Complete mandatory PSEA training
- Maintain professional boundaries with beneficiaries

Child Protection:
- Best interests of the child must be primary consideration
- Prohibition of child labor and recruitment
- Family reunification for unaccompanied minors
- Access to education and psychosocial support

Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Prevention:
- Safe and accessible services for GBV survivors
- Women's participation in program design and implementation
- Gender-sensitive needs assessments
- Confidential and survivor-centered response

DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY

Personal Data Handling:
- Obtain informed consent before collecting personal data
- Store data securely with restricted access
- Use data only for stated humanitarian purposes
- Delete data when no longer needed
- Protect beneficiary identity in case studies and reports

FINANCIAL COMPLIANCE

Anti-Money Laundering (AML):
- Know Your Customer (KYC) procedures for all transactions
- Suspicious transaction reporting
- Record-keeping for minimum 7 years
- Regular staff training on AML procedures

Counter-Terrorism Financing (CTF):
- Screening of partners and beneficiaries against sanctions lists
- Due diligence on financial transactions
- Prohibition of support to designated terrorist organizations
- Compliance with UN Security Council resolutions

ENVIRONMENTAL STANDARDS

Humanitarian operations must:
- Minimize environmental impact
- Avoid harm to natural resources
- Promote sustainable practices
- Consider climate change adaptation
- Engage communities in environmental protection

ACCOUNTABILITY TO AFFECTED POPULATIONS (AAP)

Core Commitments:
1. Information sharing: Provide accessible information about programs
2. Participation: Involve affected populations in decision-making
3. Feedback mechanisms: Establish safe and accessible complaint channels
4. Response: Act on feedback and close the feedback loop

MONITORING AND EVALUATION

Minimum Requirements:
- Baseline data collection within first month
- Regular monitoring of outputs and outcomes
- Mid-term review for projects exceeding 12 months
- End-line evaluation measuring impact
- Lessons learned documentation

REPORTING OBLIGATIONS

Incident Reporting:
Organizations must report:
- Security incidents affecting staff or operations
- Fraud, corruption, or misuse of funds
- Violations of safeguarding policies
- Serious program implementation challenges
